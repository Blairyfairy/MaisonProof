Business model reference

Commercial strategy / Investment thesis

Independent trust as a service.

A merchant-owned alternative to marketplace-mediated authentication, designed to preserve pricing autonomy, customer relationships and storefront equity.

Competitive positioning

The supplied concept benchmarks The RealReal FY2025 at $2.13B GMV, $693M revenue and a 37.7% consignment take rate. Rebag combines expert vetting, valuation and resale options. These are contextual benchmarks, not addressable revenue or independently verified market share. MaisonProof proposes a fee-for-service alternative without a sales commission.

Beachhead and distribution

Begin with independent luxury handbag merchants. Validate Shopify onboarding first, then extend the common API to Magento and WooCommerce. Prioritize merchants with repeat inventory and measurable trust-related conversion friction.

Pricing architecture

$99 per merchant per month plus $25 per reviewed item; shipping and insurance separately charged. Enterprise pricing, volume discounts and physical inspection surcharges require validation. A review fee pays for assessment, not a guaranteed approval.

Product boundary

Exact-item evidence intake, AI-assisted quality triage, qualified human decision, auditable record, storefront synchronization and public verification. Pending, revoked, expired and mismatched records must fail closed.

Unit economics and capacity

At $30/hour and 10 minutes per review, direct labor is $5 per case, leaving $20 before QA, technology, payment fees, support, insurance and claims. At scale, 360,000 reviews require 60,000 direct review hours annually (about 29 full-time equivalents at 2,080 hours, before overhead).

Capital allocation

The concept proposes $15k–$40k for a narrow pilot, not a validated production budget. Allocate funding to integration, expert calibration, security, merchant onboarding and contingency. Release further capital only after evidence of repeat demand and positive contribution margin.

Growth and retention

Track merchant activation, paid conversion, review frequency, churn, cohort retention, acquisition cost and payback. Measure conversion lift through controlled storefront experiments rather than attributing all sales changes to the badge.

Governance and validation

Proposed launch gates: zero unauthorized badge issuance in tests, complete audit records, reviewer agreement and measured turnaround. Test willingness to pay with paid pilots; monitor appeals, false approvals, reversals and customer complaints.

Illustrative stakeholder conversations

Merchant:Why not consign? — Keep your own storefront and pricing; compare total service cost and conversion outcomes against marketplace alternatives.
Investor:What supports defensibility? — Reviewer calibration, evidence provenance, integration depth and repeat merchant workflows; these are hypotheses to validate, not established moats.
Customer:Does AI certify my item? — No. A qualified human makes the decision and the public record reflects the current exact-item status.
Technical strategy / Cloud architecture

Serverless infrastructure for auditable trust.

Repository-defined architecture as depicted in the supplied diagram; live deployment and resource configuration have not been independently verified.

Ingress and identity

API Gateway HTTP API receives submissions and status queries. Cognito JWT authorization and the MaisonProofReviewers group protect reviewer decisions. Shopify webhook Lambda validates HMAC and records transactions; Secrets Manager stores shared secrets.

Asynchronous orchestration

MaisonProof API Lambda creates or updates records and issues presigned evidence upload URLs. SQS decouples intake from worker processing; retry policies and dead-letter queues isolate failures. DynamoDB transaction and record tables support idempotency and status persistence.

Evidence and AI governance

Private versioned S3 stores evidence. EventBridge routes object-created events to the evidence AI queue and Lambda. Optional Bedrock may assist image/text analysis and risk signals; it cannot approve authenticity. Treat model outputs as untrusted evidence annotations.

Human authority and security

Qualified reviewers approve or reject evidence. Persist decisions and provenance in DynamoDB. Apply least-privilege IAM, tenant isolation, scoped upload URLs, encryption, secret rotation and explicit authorization checks. Public records must exclude sensitive evidence.

Deployment and resilience

CloudFormation deploys the defined resources using S3 artifacts. CloudWatch monitors queue age, dead-letter messages, Lambda errors and duration; SNS notifications are optional. Validate backup restoration, replay safety and incident escalation before production.

Architecture trade-offs

The diagram shows a serverless baseline without VPC, EC2, RDS, CloudFront, WAF or Route 53. The SAM alternative uses API, Lambda and DynamoDB for a lower-complexity prototype. Add services only when justified by security, traffic or operational requirements.

Reliability objectives

Proposed targets: 99.9% API availability, p95 status reads below 500 ms, zero unauthorized approvals in security tests. Define queue-age alerts and recovery objectives after load and restore testing.

Operational acceptance

Test duplicate webhook delivery, replay, failed uploads, tenant boundary violations, queue redrive, expired records and reviewer access revocation. Require evidence of recovery and audit integrity before launch.

Illustrative architecture review

CTO:Why asynchronous? — Isolate traffic spikes from reviewer and evidence processing capacity; use idempotent consumers and observable queues.
